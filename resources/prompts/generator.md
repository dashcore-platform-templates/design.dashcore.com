# How to answer

You have just read the Dashcore Standard. A person will now give you a brief for one new project. Your whole job is to choose the seven decisions the standard leaves open, for that project, and to say why in one plain sentence each.

Rules for the answer:

- Return only the seven decisions in the required shape. No layout suggestions, no extra colours, no extra fonts, no component ideas. If the brief asks for something the standard forbids, do not do it; say so in the nearest `why`.
- Every value comes from the lists in the standard. `temperature`, `radius`, `density` and `motif` are the exact words listed. `pairing` is the number of an approved pairing. `accent` is a six-digit lowercase hex.
- The accent hue must be at least 30 degrees from red (0) and from amber (40), and its saturation between 45 and 85 percent. If the brief gives a `brand_colour`, use it as the accent even when it breaks that rule, and say in `why` that the brief overrides the rule.
- Read the brief for who uses the app, how often, and in what setting. Compact density is only for trained staff working all day. Warm greys suit people, health and hospitality; cool suits software and data; pure suits finance and technical work. Match the pairing's second face to the content: mono when rows carry codes, serif when trust and calm matter, display when the client has some personality. Do not pick the eyebrow motif with a pairing whose second face is not mono unless the brief is technical.
- Two different briefs should lead to different answers. Do not reach for the same accent, pairing or motif every time.
- Copy: `eyebrow` is up to four words or empty, `headline` up to eight words, `tagline` one sentence up to sixteen words, `footer` empty unless the brief asks for one. Write about the client and what the user came to do. No software words, no buzzwords, no exclamation marks, no em dashes.
- Each `why` is one sentence a person can agree or disagree with, in plain English, naming the thing in the brief that led to the choice.
